# How the shop programs: the reference library, counted

Generated by `npm run knowledge -- <library folder>` from 108 distinct programs (65 HAAS, 43 FANUC dialect) of 92 parts. The library stays outside the repository; programs are cited as `<file>:<line>` with the line's text, header lines with the part's name masked.

**Labels**, per part (a part's HAAS and FANUC programs and its other versions are one part): **consistent across the library** (every part the rule applies to follows it everywhere), **majority** (more than half of the parts), **minority** (half or fewer), **seen once** (it applies to one part). Each rule also counts the places it applies to (lines, clampings, holes...). Rules marked *(verified programs)* are read through the app's simulation and use only the 103 programs whose simulated part matches their 3D model. The data is in `knowledge.json`.

## The shop's words

- **N MM UNTERLAGE // IN Y- (or X-, Y+...)**: A spacer N mm thick in the jaw on that side, pushing the part N mm away from that jaw. Not the parallels under the part. *Told by the shop, 21.11.2026*; not written in the library's programs, the app reads it so.
- **N IN X/Y**: The part is N mm in X and N mm in Y. 50 IN X/Y, 50 x 50 mm, is one of the standard sizes of the top plates. *Told by the shop, 21.11.2026*; not written in the library's programs, the app reads it so.

Then each value on its own: the most common with its share, median and range. Depth per pass and step-over are given for milling only, the peck for drilling only. Where the tool's code is for another material, it is named. An operation's feed is the median of the feed in effect on its cutting lines: the shop's post slows arcs and corners (a finish programmed at 450 mm/min runs its corners at 315 and 284), so it can sit under the programmed feed.
